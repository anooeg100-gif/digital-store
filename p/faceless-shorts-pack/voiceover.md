# Voiceover: 3 Styles, Cold Opens, Pacing Guide

You can record every line in this pack on a phone in a closet. What changes between
videos isn't the microphone — it's the delivery style you pick before you hit record.

---

## The 3 narration styles

### Style 1 — The Explainer (default for tech, money, business, learning)
- **Tone:** clear, slightly brisk, certain — a competent colleague showing you something.
- **Pace:** **2.7–3.0 words/second** (fast but never rushed)
- **Pauses:** 0.25 s at commas, **0.5 s before any number or named rule**, 0.75 s before the CTA.
- **Delivery notes:** smile slightly while reading — it raises the pitch in a friendly way. Land hard on the *verb* and the *number*, soften the filler words. Never read the whole sentence at one volume: start mid-level, push the key phrase up ~20%, drop back down.
- **Sample lines with delivery marks** (`/` = short pause, `//` = full beat of silence, **bold** = hit):
  "Here's the math. Three hundred dollars a month, / **eight percent** a year, // **twenty years.**"
  "You're prompting it wrong — / and it's not the model's **fault**."

### Style 2 — The Confidant (relationships, habits, personal finance, creator content)
- **Tone:** lower register, conversational, like telling a friend something over coffee. Never "announcer."
- **Pace:** **2.4–2.6 words/second**, with longer gaps — you're thinking out loud.
- **Pauses:** 0.5–0.75 s between thoughts; a full **1.0 s silence after the hook** (silence after line one is the most underused hook device there is).
- **Delivery notes:** drop the volume instead of raising it on the important line — people lean into quieter speech. Read it once at the speed you'd text a friend, then record that.
- **Sample lines:** "So don't start the day negotiating with yourself. // **Five minutes tonight** — that's the whole thing."
  "I tracked it for a week, / and I hated the answer."

### Style 3 — The Storyteller (history, psychology, narrative explainers)
- **Tone:** warm, unhurried, one degree more dramatic — you know how it ends and you're letting them catch up.
- **Pace:** **2.2–2.5 words/second**; the slowest of the three on purpose.
- **Pauses:** 0.75 s before the reveal, 1.0–1.5 s at scene turns. In a story, the pause *is* the transition.
- **Delivery notes:** drop into past tense cleanly, don't speed up during the setup. Let the last word of a sentence fall down in pitch — falling endings read as truth, rising endings read as a question you're unsure of.
- **Sample lines:** "They sent two machine guns into the wheat fields of Western Australia. // **The birds did not line up.**"
  "Franklin wrote to his rival, / asked to borrow one rare book, // and got a **friend for life.**"

## The cold-open formula (first 3 seconds)

The first line must do one job: **make the next second necessary.** It has exactly
three moving parts:

```
[INTERRUPTED NORMAL] + [TWIST OR STAKES] + [SILENCE]
```

- **Interrupted normal** — name a thing the viewer already does today
  ("You walk through a door…", "Every morning you wake up and…", "You're prompting it wrong…").
- **Twist or stakes** — the sentence turns before it finishes
  ("…and forget why you came.", "…that's where the day dies.", "…and it's not the model's fault.")
- **Silence** — 0.3–1.0 s of nothing after line one. The pause is what makes the
  viewer's brain ask the question instead of you.

**Test it out loud:** if you can say the whole hook in under 3.5 seconds and the last
word lands as a *stop* (not a fade), it works. If you need a comma breath to finish it,
cut four words.

**Cold-open patterns that work in faceless formats** (pick one, don't invent a new one):
1. `Name the common behavior` → `reveal its hidden cost` — *"Rereading feels productive. It's the slowest way to learn."*
2. `State the unbelievable result first` → *"Nineteen thirty-two: the army lost to birds."*
3. `Command to stop` → *"`STOP DECIDING AT 9 A.M.`"*
4. `Straight to the number` → *"`$300/MO → $177,000`"* (say the second number out loud)
5. `Name the enemy of the viewer` → *"'You always…' — the two words that end the fight."*

## Pacing guide (words per second)

Spoken narration for faceless shorts lands at **2.2–3.0 wps**. Below 2.0 feels padded;
above 3.2 people re-watch or miss words — and rewatches are fine, but *confusion* isn't.

| Target length | Words to write | Recording math |
|---|---|---|
| **45 seconds** | **105–120 words** | 45 s × 2.6 wps ≈ 117 → write ~115, trim on read |
| **60 seconds** | **140–160 words** | 60 s × 2.6 wps ≈ 156 → write ~150, trim on read |

**Budgeting the seconds:** reserve **3.5–4 s for the hook** (text + line one + pause),
**4–5 s for the CTA/end card**, leaving the middle to carry the content. For a 45 s
video that's ~36 s of actual content ≈ 95 words of body.

**How to measure your own pace:** record yourself reading this paragraph for 15
seconds, count the words, divide by 15. Write the rest of the script at that number —
your natural pace beats every average on this page.

**Trim order when you're over time:** (1) examples and asides, (2) the second
adjective/second adverb, (3) connecting words ("actually," "basically," "so"), and
only last — never — the fact or the hook.

**Recording setup (good enough is the goal):**
- Phone 15–20 cm from your mouth, in a room with soft surfaces (a full closet is a real studio).
- Record **one line per take**, then assemble — a clean line 7 is cheaper than a perfect full read.
- Peaks around −3 dBFS, no audible background hum; normalize to about −16 LUFS in the edit.
- Same style, same distance, same room for all lines in one video — tone jumps between lines are more noticeable than any background noise.
