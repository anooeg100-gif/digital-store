# Faceless Short — Complete Master Script
## "Why You Walk Into a Room and Forget Why"

A fully written 57-second faceless short: doorway effect / memory boundaries.
Format: 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps. Narration paced at ~2.5 words/second (145 words total).
Every scene below gives exact on-screen text with timing, the narration line, the
visual you need, and the transition into the next scene. Shoot it scene by scene —
no face, no camera-op needed.

**Word timings assume a natural read.** If your narration runs long, trim adjectives,
never the fact or the hook.

---

### SCENE 1 — HOOK (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `YOU WALK IN AND FORGET WHY` (72 px, top third, pop-in 120 ms)
- **Narration:** "You walk through a door and forget why you came."
- **Visual:** Stock clip — person seen from behind pushing open a door and stopping dead in the doorway, slight hesitation. Slow 4% push-in. Film your own version if you can: phone on a tripod in the hall, you walk through and freeze.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on the word "door."

### SCENE 2 — NAME IT (0:04–0:10)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `DOORWAY EFFECT` (96 px, center, letter-spacing +10%) · `0:07.5` → `MEMORY RESETS AT THE THRESHOLD` (48 px, below it)
- **Narration:** "Psychologists call it the doorway effect: your memory resets at the threshold."
- **Visual:** Extreme close-up of a doorframe with light spilling through it — rack focus from frame to handle. Dark, moody grade so white text reads.
- **Transition:** 6-frame whip-pan right.

### SCENE 3 — THE EXPERIMENT (0:10–0:20)
- **On-screen text:** `0:10.5` → `NOTRE DAME, 2011` (56 px, top) · `0:14` → `CROSS A DOORWAY → MORE ERRORS` (56 px, center)
- **Narration:** "In 2011, Gabriel Radvansky tested this at Notre Dame. Volunteers carried objects between rooms and named what they'd moved. Cross a doorway, and their memory got worse."
- **Visual:** Three-shot sequence: (a) hands picking up a small object off a desk, (b) walking shot down a corridor, out of focus, (c) close-up of a hand hovering over printed photo cards — replace them with anything that shows "choosing."
- **Transition:** Cut on "worse."

### SCENE 4 — WHY IT HAPPENS (0:20–0:28)
- **On-screen text:** `0:20.5` → `YOUR BRAIN FILES ROOMS` (64 px) · `0:24.5` → `LEAVE ROOM → CLOSE FILE` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "His explanation: your brain files each room like a folder. Leave the room, it closes the file. Not laziness — energy saving."
- **Visual:** Overhead of labeled paper folders or a filing drawer sliding shut — practical, slightly worn office texture. If using screen capture instead: an OS file window closing. Slow slide left.
- **Transition:** Push-through (scale the shot to 150% and cut on full frame).

### SCENE 5 — THE EXCEPTION (0:28–0:36)
- **On-screen text:** `0:28.5` → `STILL OPEN IN YOUR HEAD:` (48 px, top) · `0:31` → `"GET THE MILK"` (80 px, center, quote marks included)
- **Narration:** "That's why you can forget the trip to the kitchen, but never the sentence you were halfway through. One was open — one is still open."
- **Visual:** Kitchen scene: fridge door open, light on, person standing still holding a carton and staring into space. Warm practical light. Locked-off shot.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

### SCENE 6 — THE PROOF YOU RECOGNIZE (0:36–0:45)
- **On-screen text:** `0:36.5` → `"WHY DID I WALK IN HERE?"` (64 px) · `0:41` → `SAY IT OUT LOUD` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "That's why you come back through the doorway muttering the exact same sentence — you're giving the file a label it can't drop."
- **Visual:** Person re-entering the same hallway from the opposite direction, head slightly down, shot from behind. Second half: phone recording a hand writing the sentence in a notebook.
- **Transition:** 6-frame white flash.

### SCENE 7 — THE FIX (0:45–0:52)
- **On-screen text:** `0:45.5` → `THE 2-SECOND FIX` (56 px, top) · `0:48` → `SAY WHAT YOU'RE GOING FOR — OUT LOUD` (48 px, center)
- **Narration:** "So before you cross: say the one thing you're going for, out loud. It's the cheapest memory trick there is."
- **Visual:** Clean, bright shot — hand opening a door while the other hand holds up one finger, mouth implied off-frame. Better: POV shot of a door opening with a single deliberate beat before stepping through.
- **Transition:** Cut on "is."

### SCENE 8 — CTA / END CARD (0:52–0:57)
- **On-screen text:** `0:52.5` → `ONE BRAIN TRICK A DAY` (64 px) · `0:54.5` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)
- **Narration:** "Follow for one brain trick a day."
- **Visual:** End card — solid dark background, logo/handle centered, subtle grain overlay. Hold the final 8 frames so the video loops without a black flash.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.

## Production notes for this short

- **Audio:** narration dry (no reverb), music bed at −18 dB under the voice,
  one low impact on each scene cut. Whisper-level room noise is better than a noisy
  fan — record in a closet full of clothes if your room is echoey.
- **Text rule:** never more than 2 lines × 26 characters; if it doesn't fit, cut words.
- **Caption placement:** burn your subtitles at y ≈ 1560 px so they clear this script's
  overlay text and the platform UI (see `templates.md` for layout patterns).
- **Stock license:** every stock clip here can be sourced from Pexels or Mixkit under
  free commercial licenses — sources and their terms are in `b-roll.md`.
- **Fact check before posting:** the Radvansky doorway-effect study (2011, University
  of Notre Dame) is real and widely reported — keep the year and name as written, and
  don't inflate it into "proves you have no memory."
