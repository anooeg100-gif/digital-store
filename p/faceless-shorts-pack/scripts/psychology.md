# Psychology Short — "The Favor That Makes People Like You"
**Format:** story + explanation · **Duration:** ~54 s · **Narration:** ~140 words ≈ 2.6 words/sec

## Hook (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `ASK YOUR RIVAL FOR A FAVOR` (72 px, top third, pop-in)
- **Narration:** "Ask someone who dislikes you for a favor. They'll like you more."
- **Visual:** Black-and-white still of two people on opposite sides of a hallway, slow 4% push-in. Desaturated grade sells the "old story" feel.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on "more."

## Scene 2 — The story (0:04–0:17)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `BENJAMIN FRANKLIN, 1736` (48 px, top) · `0:10` → `BORROW ONE RARE BOOK` (56 px) · `0:14` → `RESULT: LIFELONG FRIEND` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "Benjamin Franklin had a rival in the legislature who wanted him destroyed. So instead of arguing, Franklin wrote and asked to borrow a rare book from his library. The man agreed. Franklin returned it a week later with a warm thank-you. Next time they met, they were friends — and stayed friends for life."
- **Visual:** Slow pan across an old bookshelf, then a hand pulling one volume out (filmed in warm lamp light). Second half: close-up of a handwritten note on paper. All stock or home-filmed — no faces.
- **Transition:** Push-through.

## Scene 3 — Why it works (0:17–0:27)
- **On-screen text:** `0:17.5` → `THE BENJAMIN FRANKLIN EFFECT` (56 px) · `0:22` → `"I HELPED THEM → I MUST LIKE THEM"` (48 px)
- **Narration:** "Franklin wrote this down himself. Two centuries later, psychologists tested it — and found the same result: when we do someone a favor, our brain resolves the contradiction by deciding we like them. The action comes first. The feeling follows."
- **Visual:** Text-led scene over a slow-motion clip of one hand passing a book to another hand, looped. Dim, warm grade so white text reads.
- **Transition:** 6-frame white flash.

## Scene 4 — The everyday version (0:27–0:37)
- **On-screen text:** `0:27.5` → `"CAN YOU SEND ME THE LINK?"` (56 px) · `0:32` → `"QUICK OPINION ON THIS?"` (56 px) · `0:35` → `SMALL FAVOR → REAL INVESTMENT` (44 px)
- **Narration:** "You don't need a rare book. Ask a colleague for a two-minute opinion. Ask a friend to pick the restaurant. Small favors are how people quietly become part of your life — because effort turns into ownership."
- **Visual:** Three everyday cuts: (a) two coffee cups sliding across a table, (b) phone screen showing a short message being typed, (c) hands moving a menu across a counter. One second each, locked off.
- **Transition:** Hard cut per beat.

## Scene 5 — The honest limit (0:37–0:47)
- **On-screen text:** `0:37.5` → `RAPPORT, NOT MANIPULATION` (56 px, white on black — deliberately plain) · `0:42.5` → `IT HAS LIMITS` (48 px)
- **Narration:** "One honest limit: this is a rapport tool, not a control lever. It works best on neutral or mildly negative people — not on someone you've genuinely wronged. And if you're only being kind to get something, people eventually read that too."
- **Visual:** Static shot of a door closing halfway, light narrowing to a slit — the visual version of "this has limits." Slight desaturation.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## CTA (0:47–0:54)
- **On-screen text:** `0:47.5` → `ONE USABLE PSYCH TRICK PER DAY` (56 px, two lines) · `0:50.5` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)
- **Narration:** "Follow for one psychology idea you can actually use today."
- **Visual:** End card, dark background, handle centered, hold final 8 frames.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.

### Production notes
- Keep the history beats slow and the everyday beats fast — that tempo contrast is what stops the scroll mid-video.
- Layout: **Quote Frame** template for Scene 2's book scene; **Center Smash** for the one-liners.
- Music: single plucked motif under the story, cut to bare bass for Scene 5 — the honest-limit scene should feel quieter than the rest.
- The Franklin anecdote comes from Franklin's own autobiography (published 1791); the lab demonstrations date to the late 1960s. Don't attach a modern-sounding study year you can't source.
