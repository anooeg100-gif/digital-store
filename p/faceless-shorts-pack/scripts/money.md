# Money / Personal Finance Short — "The $177,000 Trick (It's Just Math)"
**Format:** number-explainer · **Duration:** ~55 s · **Narration:** ~145 words ≈ 2.6 words/sec

## Hook (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `$300/MO → $177,000` (88 px, center, pop-in 120 ms)
- **Narration:** "Three hundred dollars a month can become a hundred and seventy-seven thousand."
- **Visual:** Black screen, single number counting up fast from $0 to $177,000 in 2.5 s (animate in your editor). No footage needed — the number IS the shot.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## Scene 2 — The math (0:04–0:14)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `$300 EVERY MONTH` · `0:07.5` → `8% A YEAR (BROAD INDEX FUNDS)` · `0:11` → `× 20 YEARS` (56 px each, stacked one at a time, never all three at once)
- **Narration:** "Here's the math. Three hundred dollars a month, invested at an average eight percent a year — roughly what broad stock funds have historically returned — for twenty years."
- **Visual:** Overhead of a hand feeding three coffee-shop receipts into frame, one per text beat, then a phone screen showing an automatic transfer set up. Warm morning light.
- **Transition:** Push-through (scale to 145%, cut on full frame).

## Scene 3 — Where the money comes from (0:14–0:24)
- **On-screen text:** `0:14.5` → `YOU PUT IN: $72,000` (64 px) · `0:19` → `YOU'D HAVE: ~$177,000` (64 px, green tint) · `0:22` → `THE MARKET EARNED: $105,000` (64 px)
- **Narration:** "Over twenty years you'd have put in seventy-two thousand. Your balance would be about a hundred and seventy-seven thousand — meaning the market earned you over a hundred thousand you never worked for."
- **Visual:** Whiteboard-style counting: write the three numbers with a marker (shot from above, hand only). Keep each number on screen ≥ 1 second so it's readable silent.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on "worked for."

## Scene 4 — Why people quit (0:24–0:33)
- **On-screen text:** `0:24.5` → `YEARS 1–10: BORING` (64 px) · `0:29` → `YEARS 15–20: THE JUMP` (64 px)
- **Narration:** "Most of the growth happens in the last five years. The first ten feel boring — and that boredom is exactly where most people quit."
- **Visual:** Line chart animation, straight-ish for 70% of the width then curving up hard. Cut to a shot of someone closing a banking app with a small impatient hand gesture.
- **Transition:** 6-frame white flash.

## Scene 5 — The three rules (0:33–0:44)
- **On-screen text:** `0:33.5` → `1. AUTOMATE ON PAYDAY` · `0:37` → `2. DON'T WATCH IT DAILY` · `0:40.5` → `3. DON'T WITHDRAW EARLY` (56 px, each holds ~3 s)
- **Narration:** "Three rules make it real. Automate the transfer on payday. Don't check it daily. Don't touch it before the plan ends. Boring is the strategy."
- **Visual:** Three quick cuts of the same desk: (a) phone with transfer scheduled, (b) phone placed face-down, (c) drawer closing. One second each, locked-off camera.
- **Transition:** Hard cut per rule — the cuts land on the music.

## Scene 6 — The honest part (0:44–0:52)
- **On-screen text:** `0:44.5` → `PAST AVERAGE ≠ PROMISE` (56 px, white on black — no color, deliberately plain)
- **Narration:** "Eight percent is a long-run average, not a guarantee. Markets fall too. The real engine isn't the number — it's the years you leave it alone."
- **Visual:** Stock clip of a red down-chart day (from `b-roll.md` — money/uncertainty section), desaturated grade, slow zoom out.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## CTA (0:52–0:58)
- **On-screen text:** `0:52.5` → `ONE MONEY IDEA PER SCREEN` (56 px) · `0:54.5` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)
- **Narration:** "Follow for one money idea that fits on a screen."
- **Visual:** End card, dark background, handle centered, grain overlay, hold final 8 frames.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.
