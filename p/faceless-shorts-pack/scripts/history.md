# History / Storytelling Short — "The War Australia Lost to Birds"
**Format:** narrative story · **Duration:** ~57 s · **Narration:** ~150 words ≈ 2.6 words/sec

## Hook (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `1932: THE ARMY LOST TO BIRDS` (64 px, top third, pop-in) · `0:02` → `ON PURPOSE. ON CAMERA.` (44 px)
- **Narration:** "Nineteen thirty-two: the Australian army lost a war — to birds."
- **Visual:** Black-and-white archival-style footage of open farmland (graded to true monochrome in your editor), slow 4% push-in. No music yet — the silence sells the line.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on "birds," first drumbeat lands there.

## Scene 2 — The setup (0:04–0:15)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `WESTERN AUSTRALIA, 1932` (44 px, top) · `0:08.5` → `WWI VETERANS → WHEAT FARMERS` (48 px) · `0:12` → `20,000 EMU MIGRATION` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "After the Great War, veterans were given land in Western Australia to grow wheat. Then the emus came — around twenty thousand of them, migrating across the fields, eating the crop and walking straight through the fences."
- **Visual:** Three cuts: (a) grain silo or wheat field wide shot, (b) a farmer's hands inspecting damaged wheat (stock), (c) emu footage — walking in a flock. Keep cuts on the narration beats, each ≥ 2 seconds.
- **Transition:** 6-frame white flash.

## Scene 3 — The army arrives (0:15–0:24)
- **On-screen text:** `0:15.5` → `MAJOR G.P.W. MEREDITH` (44 px, top) · `0:19` → `2 MACHINE GUNS` (56 px) · `0:21.5` → `~10,000 ROUNDS` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "So the government did the obvious thing: they sent Major G.P.W. Meredith with two machine guns and roughly ten thousand rounds of ammunition."
- **Visual:** Slow pan over a museum-style display — belts of replica ammunition, a replica gun on a wooden table. Warm tungsten light, shallow focus. No reenactors, no weapons handling on camera beyond the still display.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## Scene 4 — Why they lost (0:24–0:37)
- **On-screen text:** `0:24.5` → `THE EMUS DID NOT LINE UP` (48 px, two lines) · `0:29` → `SMALL GROUPS · 50 KM/H` (48 px) · `0:33` → `OPERATION: CALLED OFF` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "But emus don't form ranks. They scattered into small groups, ran at fifty kilometres an hour through the bush, and the guns — mounted for a different kind of target — almost never found them. After thousands of rounds and several weeks, the operation was quietly called off."
- **Visual:** Emu running footage, cut in three accelerating clips (each starting a beat tighter than the last), intercut with one shot of dry scrubland whipping past from a moving car window. Monochrome grade throughout.
- **Transition:** Push-through.

## Scene 5 — The aftermatch (0:37–0:49)
- **On-screen text:** `0:37.5` → `HEADLINE WEEK: "WAR AGAINST THE EMUS"` (40 px, two lines) · `0:42.5` → `THE FARMERS GOT FENCES & BOUNTIES` (44 px, two lines) · `0:46.5` → `THE BIRDS WERE NEVER DEFEATED` (44 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "The press had a field day — one newspaper ran the story as a national humiliation. The minister called it a warlike operation, which didn't help. Farmers eventually got bounty payments and real fences instead. The birds went back to the wheat. Nobody ever claimed victory."
- **Visual:** Old newspaper pages fanned across a table (film a real local paper; don't fake a headline image with a real masthead), then a barbed-wire fence line receding into flat land at dusk. Cut on "wheat."
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## CTA (0:49–0:56)
- **On-screen text:** `0:49.5` → `ONE STRANGE TRUE STORY A WEEK` (44 px, two lines) · `0:52` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)
- **Narration:** "Follow for one strange, true story a week."
- **Visual:** End card, sepia-tinted dark background, handle centered, hold final 8 frames so the loop lands back on the monochrome hook seamlessly.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.

### Production notes
- Facts as written are the safe version of the story: Western Australia, 1932, Major Meredith, two machine guns, ~10,000 rounds (reports cite 9,862), operation called off, bounties and fencing followed. Don't upgrade "poorly effective cull" into "the birds outsmarted the soldiers" — the real version is already unbelievable.
- Grade: everything monochrome EXCEPT the CTA end card (sepia) — the color shift marks "now."
- Layout: **Top Third Bold** for dates and names, **Center Smash** for the three escalating beats in Scene 4.
- Music: single snare-and-strings march under Scenes 2–3, stripped to one sustained note for Scene 4 — the joke lands harder when the music is serious.
