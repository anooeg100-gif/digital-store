# Relationships / Communication Short — "Stop Saying 'You Always'"
**Format:** before/after dialogue · **Duration:** ~53 s · **Narration:** ~135 words ≈ 2.6 words/sec

## Hook (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `"YOU ALWAYS..."` (88 px, center, red-tinted) · `0:02` → `THIS ENDS THE FIGHT` (48 px)
- **Narration:** "Two words that end the conversation before it starts: you always."
- **Visual:** Two coffee cups on a table, one pushed away slightly. Locked off, shallow focus. The pushed-away cup does the acting.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on "starts."

## Scene 2 — Why it lands as an attack (0:04–0:14)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `"YOU NEVER LISTEN"` (64 px, red tint) · `0:09` → `BRAIN HEARS: VERDICT` (56 px, white) · `0:12` → `BUILDS A DEFENSE, NOT EMPATHY` (44 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "Start a sentence with you never, and the other person stops listening — not because they're childish, but because they just heard a verdict. The brain answers verdicts with defenses. Nothing gets through after that."
- **Visual:** Split-screen text beats over a static shot of a closed front door, shot from inside. Cool grade. Keep the door centered so text sits top and bottom.
- **Transition:** 6-frame whip-pan.

## Scene 3 — The swap (0:14–0:26)
- **On-screen text:** `0:14.5` → `SWAP IT:` (48 px) · `0:17` → `"I FEEL X WHEN Y BECAUSE Z"` (56 px, green tint) · `0:21` → `"I FEEL IGNORED WHEN PLANS CHANGE LAST MINUTE, BECAUSE I CLEARED MY EVENING"` (40 px, three lines, holds 4 s)
- **Narration:** "The swap: I feel X when Y because Z. Try it on the same complaint. I feel ignored when plans change last minute, because I cleared my evening. Same problem — no verdict attached."
- **Visual:** Hand writing the formula on a card, then the finished sentence placed over the same table from the hook — the cup is pushed back toward the other person. Warm light returns.
- **Transition:** Push-through.

## Scene 4 — The second tool (0:26–0:37)
- **On-screen text:** `0:26.5` → `BEFORE YOU REPLY:` (48 px) · `0:29` → `"SO THE MEETING WAS EXHAUSTING?"` (48 px, quotes) · `0:34` → `THEY RELAX THE MOMENT THEY FEEL HEARD` (40 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "Second tool: reflect before you reply. Say back the last few words you heard — so the meeting was exhausting? That's not agreeing, that's proving you were actually listening. People soften the moment they feel accurately heard."
- **Visual:** Two phones facing each other on a table (a stylized stand-in for two people talking), or footage of two people talking from `b-roll.md` (everyday-life section), shot from behind — no faces needed. Slow lateral drift.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## Scene 5 — The honest part (0:37–0:47)
- **On-screen text:** `0:37.5` → `NOT A MAGIC SPELL` (56 px, white on black) · `0:41.5` → `USE IT WHEN YOU'RE ANNOYED, TOO` (44 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "Honest part: this only works if you use it when you're the annoyed one — not just when you're winning. And no sentence formula fixes a situation where someone doesn't respect you at all. Words help; boundaries do the rest."
- **Visual:** The same door from Scene 2, now opening a hand-width with light coming through — call-back that completes the arc. Static, 1.5 seconds before the cut.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## CTA (0:47–0:53)
- **On-screen text:** `0:47.5` → `ONE CONVERSATION SKILL AT A TIME` (44 px, two lines) · `0:49.5` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)
- **Narration:** "Follow for one conversation skill at a time."
- **Visual:** End card, warm charcoal background, handle centered, hold final 8 frames.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.

### Production notes
- Red tint ONLY on the "wrong" lines and green on the "right" ones — that color code is what makes this watchable silent.
- Layout: **Center Smash** for the formula line; the long example sentence uses **Lower Third** at 40 px, max 3 lines.
- Music: quiet piano or nothing at all. This topic reads better dry.
- The I-feel-statement structure (feeling + behavior + need) comes from established communication frameworks; reflect-back is standard active listening. Present them as skills, never as therapy.
