# Learning Short — "Rereading Is the Slowest Way to Learn"

- **Narration:** "If you reread your notes and highlight them, you've picked the slowest way to learn."
- **Visual:** Close-up of a yellow marker dragging across printed text, slightly too much ink — the visual equivalent of wasted effort. Overhead, locked off.

- **Narration:** "In 2006, psychologists Roediger and Karpicke ran a simple test. One group read a passage twice. The other read it once, then closed the book and quizzed themselves. Same material. Same time spent."

- **Transition:** Push-through.

- **Narration:** "When they tested everyone a week later, the quiz group remembered more — even though rereading had felt better in the moment. Test them again a month later, and the gap was still there."

## Scene 4 — Why recalling works (0:25–0:33)
- **On-screen text:** `0:25.5` → `RECALL = THE WORKOUT` (64 px) · `0:29.5` → `REREADING = WATCHING SOMEONE LIFT` (44 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "Here's the mechanism: pulling an answer out of memory is the workout. Recognizing it on a page is watching someone else lift. One builds strength; the other just feels familiar."
- **Visual:** Cut to gym footage from `b-roll.md` (achievement section): hands chalking up or a weight being set down — never faces, never bodies mid-stride. Graded darker, one second each.
- **Transition:** 6-frame white flash.

## Scene 5 — The system (0:33–0:46)
- **On-screen text:** `0:33.5` → `CLOSE THE BOOK` (56 px) · `0:36.5` → `WRITE 3 QUESTIONS` (56 px) · `0:39.5` → `ANSWER FROM MEMORY` (56 px) · `0:42.5` → `REVIEW: +1 DAY · +3 DAYS · +1 WEEK` (44 px)
- **Narration:** "The system: close the book, write three questions, answer them from memory, check yourself. Then space the reviews — next day, three days later, a week later. Each round of retrieval makes the memory harder to forget than another pass of the highlighter."
- **Visual:** One continuous overhead take of a hand doing exactly that with a real textbook: book closes, three questions written, pen flips to check answers. Speed the writing 2×, keep the checking real-time.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

- **Narration:** "It feels worse — that struggle isn't a sign you're failing. Difficulty during retrieval is the part that does the learning."
- **Visual:** Static shot: the same textbook, closed, in a pool of lamp light. Nothing moves for two full seconds — the pause is deliberate.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.
