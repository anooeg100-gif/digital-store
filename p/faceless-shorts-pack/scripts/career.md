# Career Short — "'Tell Me About Yourself' Has a Structure"
**Format:** framework teaching · **Duration:** ~54 s · **Narration:** ~140 words ≈ 2.6 words/sec

## Hook (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `TELL ME ABOUT YOURSELF` (64 px, quotes included, top third) · `0:02` → `60 SECONDS. 3 PARTS.` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "Tell me about yourself — you have sixty seconds, and there's a structure."
- **Visual:** Empty interview chair in a small meeting room, camera slowly pulling back. Nobody in frame — the empty chair is the tension.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on "structure."

## Scene 2 — The three parts (0:04–0:15)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `1. PRESENT — WHAT YOU DO NOW` (48 px) · `0:08.5` → `2. PAST — 2 WINS THAT FIT THIS JOB` (48 px) · `0:12.5` → `3. FUTURE — WHY YOU'RE HERE` (48 px)
- **Narration:** "Present, past, future. Present: what you do right now, in one line. Past: two results that match the job you're interviewing for. Future: why that experience brought you to this table. Anything else is autobiography."
- **Visual:** Hand writing the three words on a notepad, one per beat, camera straight down. Each line stays ≥ 2 seconds so it reads on mute.
- **Transition:** Push-through.

## Scene 3 — A finished answer (0:15–0:31)
- **On-screen text:** the answer appears line by line, 44 px, white on a soft dark background: `PRESENT: "I RUN SUPPORT AT A 12-PERSON SAAS"` (0:15.5) · `PAST: "I REBUILT OUR HELP CENTER — TICKETS DOWN A THIRD"` (0:20.5) · `FUTURE: "I WANT THIS AT SCALE — THAT'S WHY I'M HERE"` (0:26)
- **Narration:** "Here's what it sounds like. Present: I run support at a twelve-person software company. Past: I rebuilt our help center and cut ticket volume by a third — and before that, four years in retail escalation. Future: I want to do this at scale, which is exactly why I'm sitting here."
- **Visual:** No new footage — build it as a clean text sequence over a dim office background loop (soft focus, one lamp). The words are the shot; keep motion minimal so people can read along.
- **Transition:** Hard cut after the last line lands.

## Scene 4 — What kills it (0:31–0:40)
- **On-screen text:** `0:31.5` → `NOT: "I GREW UP IN..."` (56 px, struck through in-editor) · `0:35.5` → `THEY HIRED A COLLEAGUE, NOT A BIO` (44 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "What kills it: the life story. Two minutes about your childhood tells them nothing about the job — and it burns the one question you were guaranteed to get."
- **Visual:** Same empty chair from Scene 1, now shot tighter, slightly lower — the interview getting uncomfortable. One slow 6% push-in.
- **Transition:** 6-frame white flash.

## Scene 5 — How to practice it (0:40–0:49)
- **On-screen text:** `0:40.5` → `RECORD IT ONCE` (56 px) · `0:44` → `RUNNING OVER 60s?` (48 px) · `0:46.5` → `CUT WHAT ISN'T ABOUT THEM` (44 px, two lines)
- **Narration:** "Record yourself once on your phone. Time it. If it runs past sixty seconds, cut every sentence that isn't about what they need — not what you want to tell them."
- **Visual:** Phone propped against a book recording a person from the chest down (face not required — hands gesturing is enough), timer visible on the phone screen counting up past 0:60.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## CTA (0:49–0:55)
- **On-screen text:** `0:49.5` → `ONE INTERVIEW ANSWER AT A TIME` (48 px, two lines) · `0:51.5` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)
- **Narration:** "Follow for one interview answer you can memorize today."
- **Visual:** End card, deep navy background, handle centered, hold final 8 frames.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.

### Production notes
- Scene 3 is the reason people share this video — give the example lines the most screen time and keep them verbatim; viewers screenshot finished answers.
- Layout: **Lower Third** for the spoken-answer lines (so they read as subtitles of a real answer), **Center Smash** for the rules.
- Music: understated corporate-neutral at −18 dB; no percussion stabs — they make job content feel shouty.
- The example answer's numbers are a sample candidate's — tell viewers in the caption to swap in their own results before they use it.
