# Health Habits Short — "The 10 Minutes After Eating"
**Format:** myth-bust + habit · **Duration:** ~55 s · **Narration:** ~140 words ≈ 2.6 words/sec

## Hook (0:00–0:04)
- **On-screen text:** `0:00.3` → `WALK 10 MIN AFTER YOU EAT` (72 px, top third, pop-in)
- **Narration:** "A ten-minute walk after eating does more than an hour at the gym — for your blood sugar."
- **Visual:** Shoes stepping out a front door into daylight, shot low from the doorstep, camera tilted up. One continuous motion — door opens, feet move, light hits.
- **Transition:** Hard cut on "sugar."

## Scene 2 — What happens after a meal (0:04–0:15)
- **On-screen text:** `0:04.5` → `AFTER EATING → BLOOD SUGAR CLIMBS` (48 px, two lines) · `0:09.5` → `THE QUESTION: HOW STEEP?` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "After any meal, your blood sugar climbs — that part is normal. The part that matters is how steep the spike gets, and how fast it comes back down. What you do in the next thirty minutes changes that curve."
- **Visual:** Simple animated line graph: one line spiking sharply, a second line rising and falling gently — draw them over a soft gradient background. Hold the second line as the narration ends.
- **Transition:** Push-through.

## Scene 3 — What the research shows (0:15–0:25)
- **On-screen text:** `0:15.5` → `STUDIES ON POST-MEAL WALKS` (48 px, top) · `0:19.5` → `10–15 MIN EASY PACE FLATTENS IT` (48 px) · `0:23` → `VS. SITTING` (56 px, lands with emphasis)
- **Narration:** "Studies comparing short post-meal walks with sitting still consistently find the walk blunts the spike — even at a gentle pace, even for ten to fifteen minutes. The size of the effect depends on the person and the meal."
- **Visual:** Footage of people walking outdoors from `b-roll.md` (nature/calm section): a park path at golden hour, shot from behind, slow lateral camera move. Cut once, mid-sentence, to a close-up of steady walking feet.
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## Scene 4 — Why walking specifically (0:25–0:35)
- **On-screen text:** `0:25.5` → `MUSCLES PULL SUGAR FROM BLOOD` (48 px, two lines) · `0:30.5` → `YOUR LEGS = A SECOND SINK` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "Here's why walking and not sitting: contracting muscle pulls glucose out of your blood with barely any insulin involved. Your legs are, functionally, a second sink for sugar — and they're idle all afternoon."
- **Visual:** Studio-style macro of a leg muscle diagram or a gym footage clip slowed to 0.4× — pick one, don't mix. Text carries the science; the footage just needs to be quiet and physical.
- **Transition:** 6-frame whip-pan.

## Scene 5 — How to actually do it (0:35–0:45)
- **On-screen text:** `0:35.5` → `AFTER YOUR BIGGEST MEAL` (48 px) · `0:39` → `TALKING PACE. NO GEAR.` (48 px) · `0:42.5` → `NOT A WORKOUT` (56 px)
- **Narration:** "How to do it so you'll actually do it: leave right after your biggest meal, walk at a pace where you could still hold a conversation, turn around and come back. No gear, no heart-rate zones, no changing clothes."
- **Visual:** Three practical shots from the same walk: (a) plate being pushed away / lunchbox closing, (b) front door closing from outside, (c) returning footsteps back up the path — same location, later light.
- **Transition:** Hard cut per beat.

## Scene 6 — The honest part (0:45–0:52)
- **On-screen text:** `0:45.5` → `NOT A PASS TO EAT ANYTHING` (44 px) · `0:49` → `EASY HABIT ≠ MEDICAL ADVICE` (44 px, plain white on black)
- **Narration:** "This isn't a license to eat whatever you want, it won't replace sleep or strength work, and it's not medical advice — if you're managing a condition, ask your doctor. It's just the lowest-friction habit available to most people."
- **Visual:** Static shot: walking shoes by the door, natural light, no movement. The stillness signals "fine print."
- **Transition:** Hard cut.

## CTA (0:52–0:58)
- **On-screen text:** `0:52.5` → `ONE HABIT YOU CAN START TODAY` (48 px, two lines) · `0:54.5` → `FOLLOW` (80 px, pill, hold to end)

- **Visual:** End card, soft green-tinted background, handle centered, hold final 8 frames.
- **Transition:** None — hold to end.

### Production notes
- Every health claim stays hedged exactly as written — the moment you say "cures" or "guarantees," the comments section becomes your fact-checker.
- Layout: **Center Smash** for the two-line claims; keep the fine-print scene at 44 px so it reads serious, not shouty.
- Music: warm and slow, −18 dB under narration; no whooshes — they make health content feel like an ad.
